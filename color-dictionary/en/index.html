Knowing a color's origin and meaning makes your palette stronger

A color dictionary collects not just color names and codes (HEX/RGB/HSL) but also the origin of each name and the meaning and impression a color carries. Knowing why a color is named the way it is lets you explain your palette in words — and a palette you can explain is far more persuasive.

This tool lets you look up, on a single screen, both Japan's 250 traditional colors with over a thousand years of history and the ~140 CSS named colors you can use directly on the web. Filter by hue or category, and click any color to see its origin, meaning, suggested pairings and contrast ratio.

Japanese traditional colors — names with a story

Japanese traditional colors were named after plant and mineral dyes, or after scenes from nature and daily life. Akane-iro is the red dyed from madder root; asagi-iro is the pale blue-green of young scallion leaves; edo-murasaki is the bluish purple favored in old Edo. To learn a name is to learn the culture the color carries.

  • From dyes and pigments: akane, kurenai, ai (indigo), gunjo, gofun
  • From plants and flowers: sakura, nadeshiko, yamabuki, sumire, fuji
  • From places, people and eras: edo-murasaki, rikyu-nezu

CSS named colors — names you can write in code

CSS defines 147 color keywords you can write instead of a HEX value, such as crimson or teal (W3C specification). Writing color: crimson; improves readability. These names have origins too: crimson comes from a deep-red dye made from the kermes insect, and rebeccapurple was added in 2014 in memory of a member of the CSS community. This tool merges spelling variants such as gray/grey into one entry.

Choosing colors for the web

  1. Pick a base color: the large background and whitespace. Low-saturation colors are easiest to work with.
  2. Pick one main color: the color that expresses your brand. Drawing from Japanese traditional colors adds originality.
  3. Pick one accent: for CTAs and emphasis. Use a small amount of a complementary or highly saturated color.
  4. Check contrast: aim for WCAG AA (4.5:1) or higher for text on background. You can verify this in each detail view.

Yes. Search by kanji name, kana, romaji, English name or HEX, and filter by hue and category.
