Image Upscaler

To enlarge a photo, load it into the tool, choose a scale and a mode, and press Enhance. Standard mode finishes in about a second; AI super-resolution mode takes longer but brings back finer detail. Every step runs inside your browser — your image is never sent to a server.

How to use it

  1. Load an image — drag and drop, or click to choose a file. JPEG, PNG, WebP and AVIF are supported.
  2. Choose a scale — anything from 1.5x to 4x. The output size and an estimated time appear right away.
  3. Choose a mode — standard mode when in doubt; AI super-resolution (2x and 4x only) when you want as much detail as possible from a clean photo.
  4. Use blur recovery only if needed — in standard mode, raise it for soft photos. Leave it off for photos that are already in focus.
  5. Compare, then save — drag the divider in the middle to compare against your browser's own enlargement, and save as PNG or JPEG.

At fit-to-screen size the difference is nearly invisible. Press 100% to view actual pixels — that is where the change in the edges becomes obvious.

The model is fetched once and served from the browser cache afterwards. Because it can render plausible detail that was not in the original, use standard mode when accuracy matters (evidence photos, measurement images and the like).

What each setting does

Blur recovery (standard mode only)

This solves the blur backwards instead of just boosting edges. It helps soft photos, but on a photo that is already sharp it makes things worse: the faint noise in any JPEG gets amplified too, and even a gentle setting lowered PSNR from 26.78 dB to 25.45 dB in our tests. The default is off. Raise it only for soft photos, while watching the comparison view.

Quality (standard mode only)

This is how many times the blur-recovery calculation repeats, so it has no effect while blur recovery is off. Higher settings work harder and take longer, and the time estimate updates whenever you change a setting.

Final sharpening (standard mode only)

A last pass that tidies up the edges. Unlike blur recovery, this only emphasises detail that is already there. Turning it up makes an image look crisper while making it slightly less faithful, so the default of about 15% is a good place to stay.

When to use it — and when not to

  • Good fit: small blog or social images you now need at print or large-display size (AI mode); slightly soft shots that need tightening (standard mode with blur recovery); small legacy images from an old site.
  • Poor fit: heavy camera shake; text that has already smeared into illegibility; rebuilding facial texture. Those need dedicated face-restoration models — the AI here is a general-purpose upscaler.

FAQ

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Everything happens in your browser. Standard mode keeps working after you disconnect; only the AI model and the AVIF encoder are fetched on first use.

How is this different from an AI upscaler?

You get both. Standard mode finishes in about a second; AI super-resolution runs a learned model inside your browser and raised PSNR from 23.24 dB to 24.02 dB on JPEG photos enlarged 4x. The model is fetched once (7.7 MB for 2x, 50 MB for 4x) and cached. In both modes your image never leaves your device.

Can it fix a blurry or out-of-focus photo?

Mild blur improves clearly with blur recovery in standard mode (+1.0 dB in our tests). Severe shake or badly missed focus will not come back.

What should I set blur recovery to?

The default is off, and off is correct for photos that are already in focus — JPEG noise gets amplified too, and even a gentle setting lowered PSNR from 26.78 dB to 25.45 dB. Raise it only for soft photos, while watching the comparison view.

Why can't I select AI mode?

AI super-resolution needs WebGPU. Where the browser does not support it, the option is disabled automatically and standard mode remains available. Recent Chrome and Edge, and Safari 18 or later, support it.

How large an image can it handle?

Up to 16 megapixels of output. In standard mode the scale is reduced automatically beyond that; in AI mode the scale is fixed, so the page tells you it cannot run.
